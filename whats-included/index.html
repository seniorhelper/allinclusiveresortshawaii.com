What's included at a Hawaii resort (and what isn't)

If you're used to all-inclusives, Hawaii resort pricing can feel like a series of add-ons. This side-by-side shows what's typically covered, so nothing surprises you at checkout.

Updated November 10, 2026

In short: a Hawaii resort rate covers your room. The resort fee adds a list of amenities that varies by property. Meals, drinks, parking, tips and most activities are paid separately. The table below shows typical patterns. Each property's own list is what counts.

What Hawaii resort fees usually cover

Many Hawaii resorts charge a mandatory daily resort fee (some call it an amenity or destination fee). Common items on these lists include:

  • Wi-Fi in rooms and public areas
  • Pool and beach towels, sometimes beach chairs or umbrellas
  • Fitness center access and some group classes
  • Cultural activities such as lei-making, hula or ukulele lessons
  • Snorkel gear or other beach equipment loans at some properties
  • Local phone calls, a welcome drink, or a discount on certain services

What resort fees almost never cover: meals, parking, spa treatments, or kids' club fees. Resort fees are taxed like the room, so factor that in (see the tax breakdown and cost estimator).

Questions to ask before you book

  1. What exactly does the resort fee include, and is it charged per room or per person?
  2. Is there a breakfast-included rate, and what's the price difference per night?
  3. Do you offer a resort credit or package this season? What are its exclusions?
  4. What does overnight parking cost, and is valet the only option?
  5. Is the kids' club included or extra, and what ages does it take?
  6. Do the rooms have a refrigerator, microwave or kitchenette?
