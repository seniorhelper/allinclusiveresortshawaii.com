All-inclusive resorts in Hawaii: what actually exists

Short answer: Hawaii has almost no true all-inclusive resorts. You can still get the same easy, prepaid, no-wallet feeling. This guide shows you how, island by island, with honest costs.

Are there all-inclusive resorts in Hawaii?

Not in the Cancún or Caribbean sense. None of Hawaii's major resort areas (Waikiki, Ko Olina, Kaanapali, Wailea, the Kohala Coast, Poipu, Princeville) runs on the classic model where one price covers your room, every meal, and unlimited drinks. Hawaii resorts sell rooms. Food, drinks, parking, and a daily resort fee are almost always extra.

What you can get: breakfast-included rates, food-and-beverage credit packages, flight-plus-hotel bundles, a week-long inter-island cruise where meals are included, or a self-catering condo you stock once. Mix two or three and you get very close.

Plenty of booking sites have an "all-inclusive" filter for Hawaii. Click into the results and you'll usually find ordinary resort rooms, sometimes with breakfast or a resort credit attached. That's not a scam, just a mismatch of labels, and it's the main reason this site exists. Read the rate details, not the filter name.

Why Hawaii doesn't do all-inclusive

Nobody publishes an official reason, but the economics are easy to see once you've been there:

  • Food costs more to get there. Most of what resorts serve is shipped or flown across the Pacific. A bottomless buffet priced like Mexico's wouldn't cover the groceries.
  • Labor costs are US costs. Hawaii hotel staff are paid on US wage scales, among the highest of any beach destination. All-inclusive margins depend on cheaper labor.
  • People leave the resort. Visitors come to drive the Road to Hana, snorkel at Hanauma Bay, see the volcano, and eat at the local plate-lunch spot. Paying for three resort meals a day you'd skip half the time is a poor deal, and resorts know it.
  • Lodging taxes stack up. In 2026 the state room tax rose to 11%, and county room tax plus general excise tax push the total on a hotel bill to roughly 18–19%. Bundling food into the room rate would mean taxing it at that rate too. (Details on our Hawaii trip cost page.)

Hawaii has had a few all-inclusive experiments over the years, usually at small, remote properties. None of the big resort areas has adopted the model. If you find a property advertising "all-inclusive," get the list of what's included in writing.

The closest things to all-inclusive in Hawaii

Each of these covers part of the all-inclusive promise. We explain how to combine them in 6 ways to go all-inclusive-style in Hawaii.

Closest overall

Inter-island cruise

Norwegian's Pride of America sails 7-night round trips from Honolulu, calling on Maui, the Big Island and Kauai. Main-dining meals are included; drinks, specialty dining and the daily service charge usually aren't.

Most common

Breakfast or resort-credit rates

Many resorts sell rates with daily breakfast or a set food-and-beverage credit. Sometimes it's a better deal than paying à la carte, sometimes it isn't. Do the math.

Best value

Condo + one big grocery run

A kitchen, a warehouse-store haul on day one, and a few planned dinners out. It's the cheapest way to stop pulling out your wallet three times a day.

Most "bubble"

Small islands and enclaves

Lanai and Ko Olina have few restaurants beyond the resorts, so most of your meals end up on-site anyway. It feels resort-contained, though you still pay per meal.

Best islands for an all-inclusive-style stay

Want the resort bubble? Oahu's Ko Olina and the island of Lanai come closest. Want a walkable strip where you can eat cheaply without a car? Waikiki and Kaanapali. Want big self-contained resorts with lots on site? The Big Island's Kohala Coast. Our island-by-island guide covers each area, plus when a rental car matters.

How much does an all-inclusive-style week cost?

It depends almost entirely on your room rate and how you eat. Rather than give a number that's wrong for you, we built a free cost estimator. Type in your actual room quote and it adds the resort fee, about 18–19% in lodging taxes, parking, a food budget and activities. It works for one person or a family of five.

Rule of thumb: the room rate you see first is rarely the room cost you pay. Add the nightly resort fee, then about 18–19% tax on both, then parking if you rent a car. Compare that total, not the headline rate.

What's included vs. what's not

A typical Caribbean all-inclusive covers meals, drinks, tips and non-motorized water sports. A typical Hawaii resort covers your room, plus whatever its resort fee lists, often Wi-Fi, pool towels, some fitness classes and cultural activities, and sometimes snorkel gear. Families, couples and adults-only

Families usually do best with a lagoon or calm-water beach, a kids' club and a kitchenette. Couples who want quiet have a short list of adults-only options, including Hotel Wailea on Maui and Sensei Lanai. See Hawaii resorts for families vs. couples.

All-inclusive Hawaii: quick answers

Are there any all-inclusive resorts in Hawaii?

Essentially none in the classic sense, where one price covers your room, all meals and drinks. Hawaii resorts sell rooms, and food, drinks, parking and resort fees are usually extra. Some properties sell breakfast-included rates or food-and-beverage credit packages, and a few small luxury properties bundle more than most. If a listing says "all-inclusive," ask for the written list of inclusions before you book.

Why doesn't Hawaii have all-inclusive resorts like Mexico or the Caribbean?

Mostly economics. Food has to be shipped to the islands, staff earn US wages, lodging taxes on the room bill total roughly 18–19% in 2026, and most visitors leave the resort to explore and eat elsewhere. All-inclusive pricing depends on low food and labor costs and guests who stay on property, and Hawaii offers neither.

What is the closest thing to an all-inclusive vacation in Hawaii?

A 7-night inter-island cruise on Norwegian's Pride of America is the closest single product: your cabin, main-dining meals and transport between four islands are bundled, though drinks, specialty restaurants, shore excursions and the daily service charge are typically extra. On land, a breakfast-included or resort-credit rate in a self-contained area like Ko Olina or Lanai gets you close.

Is a Hawaii cruise all-inclusive?

Partly. Standard cruise fares include your cabin, meals in the main dining rooms and buffet, basic drinks like water and coffee, and entertainment. Alcohol, specialty dining, Wi-Fi, excursions and the daily service charge are usually extra unless you buy a package. Check the cruise line's current inclusions for your sailing date.

How much does an all-inclusive-style week in Hawaii cost?

There is no honest single number, because room rates swing widely by island, season and resort. The reliable method: take your nightly room quote, add the resort fee, add about 18–19% tax on both, then add parking, food and activities. Our free cost estimator does that math with your own numbers.

Which Hawaiian island is best for an all-inclusive-style trip?

For a resort-bubble feel, Oahu's Ko Olina and the island of Lanai, where most meals end up being at the resort anyway. For walkable, flexible eating without a car, Waikiki on Oahu or Kaanapali on Maui. For large resorts with lots to do on site, the Kohala Coast on the Big Island.

Do Hawaii resort fees include meals?

Rarely. Resort fees usually cover things like Wi-Fi, pool and beach towels, fitness classes, cultural activities and sometimes snorkel gear or a welcome drink. Meals are almost never included. Each resort publishes its own list. Read it before booking, and remember resort fees are taxed along with the room.

Are there adults-only resorts in Hawaii?

Only a few. Hotel Wailea on Maui is adults-only, and Sensei Lanai on the island of Lanai restricts younger guests (listings give different minimum ages, so confirm with the resort). Many large family resorts also have an adults-only pool area. None of them is a classic all-inclusive.
