What does an all-inclusive-style Hawaii trip cost?

The headline room rate is only the start. Here's how the real total builds up in 2026, with a calculator that uses your own numbers instead of made-up averages.

Updated November 10, 2026

The honest formula: (nightly room rate + daily resort fee) × nights × about 1.18–1.19 for taxes, plus parking, food, and activities. Food is the biggest swing: a family eating every meal at resort restaurants can spend more on food than on the room.

Hawaii lodging taxes in 2026

On January 1, 2026, Hawaii's state Transient Accommodations Tax (TAT) rose from 10.25% to 11%. This is the increase often called the "green fee." Each county adds its own room tax, and the general excise tax (GET) applies too. Here's how it stacks on a hotel bill:

Two things trip people up. First, resort fees are part of the taxable lodging charge, so they get the full tax too. Second, quotes on third-party sites sometimes show "taxes and fees" as one lump that may or may not include the resort fee. Check the final payment screen.

The other costs that surprise people

  • Resort fees. Charged per night at many Hawaii resorts, commonly in the tens of dollars, and they rarely include food. See what resort fees typically cover.
  • Parking. Resort and Waikiki hotel parking is charged per night, often heavily. If you only need a car for a day or two, rent for those days only.
  • Restaurant prices. Most food is shipped in, and resort restaurants price accordingly. A breakfast-included rate or a kitchen can save a lot. See the six ways to get close to all-inclusive.
  • Gratuities. Unlike most all-inclusives, Hawaii restaurants, tours, valets and housekeeping expect tips, just like the mainland US.
  • Park and activity fees. Some popular state and national parks charge entry or parking fees, and some require advance reservations. Check the official park site before you go.

How Hawaii compares with a Mexico or Caribbean all-inclusive

Comparing headline prices is misleading because the products differ. A Caribbean all-inclusive price usually already covers meals, drinks and tips. A Hawaii room rate covers only the room. To compare fairly, take the Hawaii room total from the estimator above, add your food and drink figure, and set that against the all-inclusive's total for the same nights, including its own taxes and any transfers.

Hawaii trip cost questions

What is the hotel tax in Hawaii in 2026?

About 18–19% in total on the room bill. That's the 11% state Transient Accommodations Tax (up from 10.25% on January 1, 2026), a 3% county room tax, and the general excise tax of 4% plus a 0.5% county surcharge, which businesses may pass on at up to about 4.712%. The tax applies to the resort fee as well as the room rate.

Are resort fees taxed in Hawaii?

Yes. Mandatory resort fees are treated as part of the charge for the room, so the same lodging taxes apply to them. A $45 nightly resort fee costs roughly $53 a night once taxes are added. Always compare the total including taxes and fees, not the base rate.

How much should I budget for food in Hawaii per day?

It depends far more on where you eat than on the island. Eating every meal at resort restaurants can cost several times as much as a mix of groceries, plate lunches and casual spots. Our estimator uses rough planning figures for three eating styles that you can adjust. The most reliable way to budget is to look up the menus of the resort restaurants where you're staying.

Do I need to tip in Hawaii?

Yes. Hawaii follows mainland US tipping customs: restaurant servers, bartenders, tour guides, boat crews, valets and housekeeping all generally expect tips. That's a real difference from many Caribbean and Mexican all-inclusives, where gratuities are often built into the price.

Is Hawaii more expensive than an all-inclusive in Mexico?

Usually, once food and drinks are added, though it depends on the resort and season. Hawaii room rates rarely include meals, lodging taxes total about 18–19%, and restaurant prices are high because most food is shipped in. A fair comparison sets the full Hawaii total (room, fees, taxes, food, drinks, tips) against the all-inclusive's total price.
