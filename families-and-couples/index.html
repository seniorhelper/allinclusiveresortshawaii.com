Hawaii resorts for families vs. couples (and adults-only options)

What makes a Hawaii stay feel all-inclusive is different for a family of five than for a honeymooning couple. Here's what to look for, and the honest list of adults-only options.

Updated November 10, 2026

Families: prioritize calm water, a kitchenette, and a resort area where kids can do a lot without the car. Couples: look at adults-only properties (there are only a few), quieter resort areas, and resort-credit packages that cover a couple of nice dinners.

For families

What matters most

  • Calm, protected water. Hawaii's surf and currents can be strong, especially in winter on north-facing shores. Lagoons and reef-protected beaches are easier with small kids. Ko Olina's lagoons on Oahu are a well-known example. Always check lifeguard flags and ocean conditions, and swim near lifeguards where possible.
  • A kitchen or kitchenette. Feeding kids three resort meals a day is where family budgets fall apart. Villa-style and condo units with kitchens, common at Ko Olina, Kaanapali and on the Big Island, make breakfast and lunch nearly free by comparison.
  • Kids' club details. In Hawaii, kids' clubs usually charge a separate fee, and age ranges, hours and potty-training rules vary. Ask before you count on one.
  • Lots on site. Big family resorts with pools, waterslides, lagoons, and some way to get around the grounds make the "we never had to leave" feeling possible.

Family-friendly resort areas

Ko Olina (Oahu) for lagoons and a self-contained feel. Kaanapali (Maui) for a walkable beach strip with lots of food choices. The Kohala Coast (Big Island) for large resorts with lagoons and plenty of on-site activities. Poipu (Kauai) for a sunny base with kid-friendly beaches nearby. Compare them in our island-by-island guide.

Some family resorts run seasonal kids-eat-free or breakfast-included promotions. They come and go, so check the resort's own offers page when you're ready to book.

For couples

Adults-only options in Hawaii

True adults-only resorts are rare in Hawaii. The ones most often cited:

  • Hotel Wailea (Maui). A small, adults-only boutique hotel above the Wailea coast. Booking listings show an 18-and-over policy.
  • Sensei Lanai, A Four Seasons Resort (Lanai). A wellness-focused retreat in Lanai's uplands that restricts younger guests. Published minimum ages differ between listings, so confirm the current policy directly with the resort.

Neither is a classic all-inclusive, though luxury properties like these sometimes offer packages that bundle dining, spa or activities. Ask what's available for your dates.

Other ways to get an adults-only feel

  • Choose a resort with a dedicated adults-only pool. Many large Hawaii resorts have one, and it changes the feel of a family resort completely.
  • Pick quieter areas such as Kapalua or Wailea on Maui, Lanai, or the Kohala Coast rather than the busiest family strips.
  • Travel outside school-holiday periods when fewer families are visiting.
  • Use a resort-credit package for two or three special dinners and keep the rest of your eating simple.

Honeymoons

If what you really want is a no-decisions honeymoon, a two-island trip often works better than trying to recreate an all-inclusive: a few lazy days at one resort with a breakfast-included rate, then a short flight to a second island with a completely different feel. Lanai pairs naturally with Maui. If you'd rather unpack once, the inter-island cruise option covers four islands in a week.

Whatever you choose, run the numbers in the cost estimator and check the resort-fee inclusions first.
